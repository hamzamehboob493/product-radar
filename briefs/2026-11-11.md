### D1. Track A: Airtable exit-ready Postgres mirror (60, WATCHLIST)

**1. Idea and buyer.** Web app. Ops leads and no-code agencies in the US, UK, EU and AU running business-critical Airtable bases (CRM, inventory, content ops). Recurring workflow: keeping a usable, queryable copy of the data outside Airtable, including attachments, so the business is not stuck if prices or limits change. Who pays: the ops lead or the agency on a client's behalf. Why now: Bending Spoons closed the acquisition on 4 Sep and large layoffs followed on 15 Oct [F]([InformationWeek](https://www.informationweek.com/it-strategy/airtable-s-post-acquisition-layoffs-put-vendor-risk-in-focus)); the article advises CIOs to "prepare an exit option" [F]. Airtable said on 12 Oct that plans and pricing were unchanged [R]([Teable](https://teable.ai/blog/airtable-acquisition-bending-spoons)). Smallest promise: "Your Airtable data, attachments included, in your own Postgres, refreshed hourly, ready to leave."

**2. Proven model and angle.** ProBackup sells Airtable backups at $25, $38 or $69 a month billed annually and claims "over 3,000 companies" [F, vendor claim]([ProBackup](https://www.probackup.io/compare/probackup-vs-on2air-for-airtable)); On2Air sells at $49.99 and $79.99 a month [R, per ProBackup]. Whalesync sells two-way Airtable and Postgres sync at $16 or $32 a month billed yearly [F]([Whalesync](https://whalesync.com/pricing)). What is missing: ProBackup's comparison page shows no Postgres export for either backup tool [F]; Whalesync does not market attachment archiving or an exit [I]. No review quotes were collected this run [U]. Our angle: backup plus mirror plus attachment files plus SQL views for formulas and lookups. Copy risk: high; Whalesync or ProBackup could add the missing piece in weeks [I].

**3. Evidence with numbers.**
