### 1. California AI provenance kit (SB 1000) · 64/100 · WATCHLIST (1 of 2)
**Track:** Micro (build ≤5 days, launch by day 10) · **Sector:** Compliance
**What it is:** One API for small AI image apps: sign each output with C2PA Content Credentials, add an invisible watermark, and host the free public verification page the law asks for. Audio and video later.
**Who pays:** Indie and seed-stage AI image, avatar and video apps (1 to 20 people) available in California; EU apps next.
**The problem:** SB 1000 removed the 1M-user threshold. Every public generative AI system must embed a permanent latent disclosure (provider, version, timestamp, ID) and offer a free verification tool [F] <https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/261001-california-revamps-ai-transparency-requirements>. $5,000 per violation, each day separate [F] <https://thebuild.com/blog/sb-1000-everyone-is-a-covered-provider-now/>
**Why now:** In force 30 Sep 2026 as an urgency measure [F] <https://wsgr.com/en/insights/california-enacts-over-two-dozen-key-privacy-and-ai-bills-into-law-in-2026-legislative-session.html>
**Market analysis:**
- Reachable apps: unknown. Whether apps that wrap OpenAI or Google APIs count: unknown.
- Money flowing: Steg.AI enterprise plan $250,000/year [F] <https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-5l3hu3ljm7dju>. No small-developer product found.
- Competitors: Adobe TrustMark (free, images only, removable) [F]; C2PA SDKs (free, need hosting); Truepic and Digimarc (enterprise, prices unknown).
- Gap: nothing between free libraries and enterprise contracts.
**Our angle:** "Signed, watermarked, verifiable in one call", priced for indie apps.

### 2. Salesforce Quip exit migrator · 63/100 · WATCHLIST (1 of 2)
**Track:** Bigger build (1 to 4 weeks, launch by day 30) · **Sector:** Productivity
**What it is:** Connect a Quip site, get a free audit of what the native export will lose, then export docs, spreadsheets, folders and full comment threads to Confluence, Notion, Google Docs or DOCX/Markdown for a flat price.
**Who pays:** Salesforce admins and IT leads at companies on Quip, US and UK first.
**The problem:** No Quip renewals after 1 Mar 2027, then read-only, then blocked logins and deletion [F] <https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-announces-end-of-life-for-quip/>. The API returns only the latest 25 comments per doc, live apps don't export, Salesforce mentions come out blank [F] <https://clonepartner.com/blog/where-to-move-documents-after-quip-retires-2027-decision-guide/>
**Why now:** Last renewal 1 Mar 2027; big migrations take 3 to 6 months [F].
**Market analysis:**
- Quip customer count: unknown.
- Money flowing: ClonePartner charges $1/page after 10 free [F]; Swiftix Quip to Confluence, paid, 15 installs [F] <https://marketplace.atlassian.com/apps/1231437/quip-to-confluence-integration-and-migration>
- Competitors: ClonePartner (per page, service-led); Swiftix (Confluence only); Salesforce Quip to Slack canvas (free, no spreadsheets); quip-export (open source, stale since 2023).
- Gap: self-serve, any destination, comments kept, flat price.
**Our angle:** Free loss audit first; $999 flat versus ~$4,990 per-page for a 5,000-page site [E].

**Economics [E]:** ~$800 build; recovered by ~2 services or ~17 kits. One-off revenue.
**Validation test:** 5 days, ≤$60: pre-order page, one SEO article, $50 Google Ads on "zendesk api token oauth". Go: 3+ paid pre-orders. No-go: under 2 pre-orders and under 10 signups.
**Risks:** Developers find it trivial; Zendesk adds webhook OAuth.
**Confidence:** Low on willingness to pay.
