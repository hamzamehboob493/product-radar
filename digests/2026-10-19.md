### 1. Returning: UK motor-finance redress request desk for dealers · 61/100 · WATCHLIST (card 1 of 3)
**Track:** Bigger build (build 1 to 4 weeks, launch by day 30) · **Sector:** Automotive
**What it is:** a web app where a car dealer or credit broker logs each lender request for old finance records, matches it to scanned files and DMS exports, and sends an evidence pack (or a "not held" letter) before the 1-month deadline. It tracks requests and assembles documents. It gives no advice on liability.
**Who pays:** UK franchised and independent car dealer groups and motor credit brokers, 1 to 20 rooftops.
**The problem:** the FCA says brokers "must give lenders requested documents or confirm they don't hold them within 1 month", even though compensation is paused <https://fca.org.uk/news/statements/motor-finance-scheme-partially-suspended>. The FCA flagged as weak practice that "firms defer key decisions on broker engagement until late" (Forvis Mazars, 18 Sep 2026) <https://www.forvismazars.com/uk/en/insights/financial-services-insights/motor-finance-consumer-redress-for-dealers>. Lender requests can arrive in batches of about 500 records <https://www.restore.co.uk/informationmanagement/resource-hub/insights/how-dealers-should-prepare-for-motor-finance-redress-delivery/>.
**Why now:** lenders must notify post-2014 complainants by 18 Nov 2026 and pre-2014 ones by 18 Jan 2027. The tribunal hearing is 14 to 18 Dec 2026 (or Feb 2027). Final complaint date 31 Aug 2027.

### 1. Motor-finance redress request desk (card 2 of 3)
**Market analysis:**
- Market size: 12.1M eligible agreements, £9.1bn total cost <https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/fca-publishes-final-rules-for-motor-finance-redress-scheme-what-s-changed-what-stays-the-same-and-what-firms-should-do-now/>. About 28,000 firms hold credit-broking permission across all sectors, Q1 2026; the motor share is unknown <https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/specialist-lending/commercial-finance/2026/06/26/fca-data-shows-credit-broker-permissions-shrink-45-since-pandemic/>
- Money already flowing: Restore (records retrieval), FTI (lender-side portal) and consultancies such as Forvis Mazars, Grant Thornton and MHA all sell into this scheme. None publishes prices
- Competitors: Restore (custom quote, scanning-led); FTI (built for lenders); consultancies (day rates); the manual route of a DMS export, a spreadsheet and email (free but slow)
- The gap: no self-serve tool for the dealer side with public pricing was found
**Our angle:** a £149/month tool that a dealer's finance manager can set up in an afternoon, with no consultancy project.
**Build difficulty:** Medium · 12 to 15 engineer-days (AI-assisted) · Next.js, FastAPI, Postgres, S3 (London region), OCR via Textract or an LLM · hardest part: matching messy 2007 to 2024 scans to agreements by name, registration and date · about 10 h/month upkeep
**Where to publish and sell:**
- Main: own site with Polar checkout; no marketplace review
- Secondary: LinkedIn (dealer-group compliance and finance directors), NFDA and independent dealer groups, Car Dealer Magazine and AM readers
- Payment: Polar, 5% + 50¢ plus 1.5% on non-US cards. Pakistan payouts are listed (baseline Sep 2026) but untested

### 1. Motor-finance redress request desk (card 3 of 3)
**Pricing and economics (estimates):** £149/month per rooftop, or £990/year. Running cost at 50 customers about £250/month (hosting plus OCR). Margin about 88% after fees. Break-even at 2 to 3 customers on running cost. 25 customers ≈ £3,700/month; 100 ≈ £14,900/month.
**Launch plan:** D0 confirm · D1 to D5 validation test · D7 go/no-go and sign 2 design-partner dealers · D8 to D21 request inbox, matcher and pack generator · D22 to D27 QA on partner data, DPA and security pack · D28 to D30 launch.
**First 10 customers:** 30 LinkedIn DMs to compliance and finance leads at independent dealer groups; NFDA and UK motor-trade groups. Pitch: "Answer every lender records request inside the 1-month deadline, with an audit trail, for £149 a month."
**Validation test (before building):** landing page with a £1 Polar pre-order or "book a demo", 30 DMs and 1 group post, 5 days, under $60. Go: 4 calls booked or 2 paid pre-orders. No-go: fewer than 2 replies saying they have already had lender requests.
**Risks:**
- The tribunal quashes the scheme in Dec or Feb (mitigation: lenders still need records for ordinary complaints and FOS cases)
- UK dealers may not trust a Pakistan vendor with customer data (mitigation: UK hosting, DPA, IDTA, no offshore data access)
- Copy that drifts into "assess liability" triggers K2 (mitigation: process-only wording)
**Confidence:** Medium-low. The obligation is real and dated, but dealer demand for a tool is unproven.

### 2. Zendesk Sell full-history archive · 54/100 · WATCHLIST (card 1 of 3)
**Track:** Micro (build ≤5 days, launch by day 10) · **Sector:** Sales and revenue ops
**What it is:** connect a Zendesk Sell API token and download everything Sell will delete. That covers calls, visits, text messages, notes, tasks and documents as well as leads, contacts and deals. You get a ZIP of CSV and JSON plus an offline HTML viewer with a timeline per customer.
**Who pays:** SMB sales teams still on Zendesk Sell (US, UK, EU, AU), especially those moving to a CRM that Import2 does not cover or that must keep records.
**The problem:** Zendesk says "activity history, appointments, emails, call logs, and documents cannot be exported", and deletion "is permanent and cannot be reversed" <https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/9591462550042>. Export files last only 30 days <https://www.salesforceben.com/zendesk-sell-is-being-retired-what-you-need-to-know/>.
**Why now:** Sell retires 31 Aug 2027, and data goes earlier if the subscription ends first. Announced Sep 2025, so many accounts have already moved.

### 2. Zendesk Sell full-history archive (card 2 of 3)
**Market analysis:**
- Market size: number of remaining Sell accounts unknown; Capterra shows 160 reviews <https://www.capterra.com/p/227019/Zendesk-Sell>. Estimate: 2,000 to 5,000 live accounts, 1 to 3% buyers
- Money already flowing: Import2 has 8K HubSpot installs and costs from $99 one-time <https://ecosystem.hubspot.com/marketplace/listing/import2datamigration>; Faye Digital sells a Sell-to-Pipedrive utility; SpotDev migrations "from £5,000" (snippet)
- Competitors: Import2 (free into Pipedrive up to 1M records; exports calls with recordings, notes, tasks and attachments, but not emails or meetings <https://help.import2.com/en/articles/8512312-what-objects-are-supported-for-zendesk-sell-migrations>); Nutshell (free import plus 80% off); Pipedrive's CSV guide (free, loses calls and documents)
- The gap: none of these keeps a standalone, searchable archive, or exports visits and text messages
**Our angle:** "Keep everything, whatever CRM you pick, or none." A one-time archive for record-keeping, not a migration.
**Build difficulty:** Easy to Medium · 5 engineer-days (AI-assisted), archive only · Node or Python worker, Sell Core API, S3 · hardest part: rate limits and document links that expire after 90 minutes · 2 to 3 h/month
**Where to publish and sell:**
- Main: own site with Polar checkout; no review wait
- Secondary: Zendesk community threads, SEO for "zendesk sell export call history", partner CRMs not covered by Import2's Pipedrive offer (Attio, Capsule, Streak)
- Payment: Polar, one-time; Pakistan payouts listed but untested

**Risks:**

- We have no Sell test account, and new trials may be closed (mitigation: a design partner's token)

**Confidence:** Low. The deletion is forced, but cheaper tools already cover most of it.
